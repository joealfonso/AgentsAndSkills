# Survey design

**Use when:** you need answers from more people than you can interview

**You'll need:** what you need to learn, who you'll survey, how you'll reach them

## Prompt

```text
Design a short survey.

What I need to learn: [QUESTIONS]
Audience: [WHO], reached via [CHANNEL]
How results will be used: [DECISION]

1. Tell me first if a survey is the wrong tool for any of these questions (why-questions usually need interviews), and what to use instead.
2. 8-12 questions maximum, each with: the question, the answer format, and which learning goal it serves. Cut anything that serves none.
3. Avoid leading wording, double-barreled questions, and "would you use" hypotheticals. Ask about recent, specific behavior.
4. Include 2-3 open-ended questions, placed right after the closed questions they relate to ("Anything about that we didn't ask?"). These are where unexpected answers show up.
5. Screening and segment questions, so results can be split by the groups that might answer differently.
6. An analysis plan: how closed questions get summarized, and how open answers get read. Every open answer gets read in full, not keyword-counted, and rare answers are reported as outliers rather than dropped.
7. Who will likely not respond, and how that biases the results.
```
