Scope Design-layer review
What this instrument is for. Most AI governance frameworks operate at the organizational or model layer. Very few translate into decisions a designer makes about a specific screen. This instrument sits in that gap: it takes obligations and findings from NIST AI RMF, the EU AI Act, the Microsoft HAX guidelines, Google PAIR, ISO/IEC 42001, and WCAG, and expresses them as reviewable properties of an interface. It is a design-layer instrument, it does not assess model performance, data governance, or legal compliance, and it is not a substitute for a formal conformity assessment or legal review.

Section 2

Scoring method

Scale. Each criterion is scored 0 (not met), 1 (partially met), or 2 (met, with evidence). N/A removes a criterion from both the numerator and denominator. A score of 2 requires a stated artifact, test result, or observation in the evidence field, not reviewer impression. This is the primary control on inter-rater drift.

Three independent conditions must all hold for a Ready verdict. A single aggregate percentage can hide a systemic failure inside a good average, so it is deliberately not the only test.

  1. Composite threshold — total score meets the tier minimum.
  2. Dimension floor — no single dimension falls below the tier floor. Prevents one neglected area being averaged away by strength elsewhere.
  3. Gate clearance — every gate criterion active at this tier is satisfied. Gates encode obligations that cannot be traded off, largely derived from EU AI Act Articles 13, 14 and 50. A failed gate returns Blocked regardless of composite score.

Verdicts. Ready: all three conditions met. Conditional: composite met, one other condition failed, re-review required after remediation. Not ready: composite below threshold. Blocked: an active gate scored 0 at Tier 2, or below 2 at Tier 3.
